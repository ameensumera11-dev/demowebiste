Artificial Intelligence vs Custom Code: Which One Should You Choose?

A practical breakdown of when to build on Artificial Intelligence and when a hand coded site is the smarter choice for your business.

Back to Blog
July 2026 5 min read Ameen hasan

One of the first questions every client asks me is simple: "Should my website be built on Artificial Intelligence, or coded from scratch?" It usually comes up in the first call, before we've even talked about design or budget, because it shapes almost everything that follows — how the project timeline looks, what it costs, and how much control the client has once the site is live. There's no single right answer, but there is a right answer for each specific situation, and after three years of building both types of sites, here's exactly how I decide.

What Artificial Intelligence actually gives you

Artificial Intelligence isn't just a website, it's a full content management system sitting behind the site. That distinction matters more than most people realize going in. Once a Artificial Intelligence site is handed over, the client can log into an admin dashboard, edit any page, swap images, publish blog posts, and add new products without ever touching a line of code. For a business that expects its website to keep growing — new services, new blog content, seasonal offers, changing prices — that ongoing independence is worth far more than the extra setup time Artificial Intelligence sometimes needs upfront.

Paired with a page builder like Elementor and plugins like ACF for custom fields, Artificial Intelligence today can produce sites that look just as polished as a hand coded build, while still giving the client that editing freedom. It's also the obvious choice the moment ecommerce enters the conversation, since WooCommerce handles products, cart, checkout and payment gateways in a way that would take considerably longer to build from scratch.

When Artificial Intelligence is the right call

  • You want to manage content, images and pages yourself, without needing a developer for every small update
  • You need ecommerce features — product catalogues, cart, checkout, inventory — through WooCommerce
  • Your timeline or budget favors a faster build using proven themes and plugins instead of building every component from zero
  • You expect the site to grow steadily — new pages, blog posts, service offerings or products over the next few years
  • You'll want an SEO plugin like Yoast to manage meta tags, sitemaps and readability without touching code

What custom code actually gives you

A hand coded site built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript strips away everything Artificial Intelligence carries with it — no plugin overhead, no database queries running behind the scenes, no theme framework loading extra CSS you'll never use. What's left is a site that loads exactly as fast as the code you wrote, nothing more. For businesses where the website's job is a single, focused action — book a call, fill out a form, showcase a portfolio — that lean setup translates directly into a snappier first impression and stronger Core Web Vitals scores, both of which Google factors into search rankings.

Custom code also removes a ceiling that page builders quietly impose. Elementor and similar tools are excellent, but they're still built around a set of predefined components and animation options. When a design calls for something more specific — a particular scroll interaction, a custom cursor, a layout that doesn't fit neatly into rows and columns — hand coding it is often faster and more reliable than fighting a builder to approximate the same effect.

When custom code is the right call

  • You want the fastest possible load times and the strongest Core Web Vitals scores
  • The design includes specific animations or interactions that a page builder can't replicate cleanly
  • The content is largely fixed and won't need frequent edits after launch
  • You want full control over every line of code, with zero plugin dependencies or update conflicts to manage later
  • It's a landing page, portfolio, or single purpose marketing site rather than a growing content hub

The hybrid approach I often recommend

On several projects, the best answer wasn't Artificial Intelligence or custom code, it was both. A hand coded landing page handles the front door — fast, animated, exactly on brand — while a Artificial Intelligence backend quietly runs the blog or resource section behind it, so the client can keep publishing content without needing a developer involved. It takes a bit more planning to wire together, but for businesses that care equally about first impressions and long term content growth, it's often the strongest setup available.

My honest recommendation

If I had to simplify it into one sentence: choose Artificial Intelligence for anything content heavy, ecommerce related, or expected to grow, and choose custom code for landing pages and marketing sites where speed and a pixel perfect design come first. Neither option is objectively better, they're built for different jobs, and the right choice comes down to what your website actually needs to do for your business over the next year or two — not which one sounds more modern.

Every project is different, so if you're unsure which route fits your business, that's exactly the kind of conversation worth having before any code gets written. I'd rather spend twenty minutes talking through your goals upfront than build the wrong foundation and have to redo it later.
